Dr. Strange

Brings the magical world of Dr. Strange to life by allowing users to generate and display real-time, interactive shields similar to those seen in the Dr. Strange movies.

Overview

Dr. Strange Shield turns a webcam into a virtual camera that overlays the Sorcerer Supreme's mystical shields onto your hands in real time, so the effect can be used live in any video-conferencing app.

A short sequence of hand poses acts as the spell: three gestures in order summon the shields, and a fourth dismisses them. Once armed, a shield video is composited on each hand and follows it as it moves, rotates or changes distance from the camera.

Two shields can appear at once, one per hand, exactly as in the films.

What it does

Tracking

  • Real-time tracking of both hands
  • Shields resized and repositioned every frame to match the hand
  • Clipping handled at the edges of the frame

Spell casting

  • Gesture sequence recognised by a trained classifier
  • Adjustable confidence threshold
  • Live status of the shield and the gesture progress

Output

  • Virtual camera selectable in any conferencing app
  • Preview window, virtual camera or both

How it is built

A MediaPipe hand tracker extracts the landmarks, and a scikit-learn support-vector classifier recognises the hand poses. OpenCV composites a chroma-keyed shield video onto the frame, and the result is published through a virtual camera backend.

Tech stack

Python

Glues together video capture, hand-pose classification, frame compositing, and the virtual camera output into a single real-time loop.

MediaPipe

Provides the HandLandmarker task used for two-hand detection, returning 21 3D landmarks per hand every frame — the basis for both the gesture classifier and the shield anchoring.

Sklearn

Used to train and load the SVM model on normalized hand-landmark coordinates, and to expose per-class prediction probabilities for confidence-gated gesture recognition.

SVM

The classifier itself: a support vector machine trained to recognise the four hand poses (three "keys" plus a dismiss gesture) that drive the shield state machine.

OpenCV

Handles webcam capture, chroma-key masking of the shield video against a black background, and per-frame alpha compositing onto the live hand region.

pyvirtualcam

Streams the composited output frames to the OBS Studio virtual camera device, exposing the effect as a selectable webcam in third-party video-call software.
