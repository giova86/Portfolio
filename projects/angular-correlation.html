Angular Correlation

Analyzes and simulates the directional relationship between two emitted gamma rays in nuclear decay processes, visualizing the complex physics behind these phenomena.

Python Streamlit Nuclear Physics Matplotlib
Live Demo

Overview

Angular Correlation is a Streamlit application for computing, plotting and fitting γ-γ angular correlations in nuclear physics. When an excited nucleus de-excites through a cascade of two gamma rays, the direction of the second photon is not isotropic with respect to the first: the angular distribution W(θ) encodes the spins of the three nuclear levels involved and the multipolarities of the two transitions. This app turns that measurement into an interactive workflow — declare the decay scheme, get the theoretical correlation, upload the experimental counts, and read off the fitted coefficients.

The interface is split into three tabs. Theory is a full write-up of the formalism, rendered in LaTeX: the Legendre expansion of W(θ), the truncation rule for kmax, the definition of the Akk coefficients as a product of the two Ak, the mixing ratio δ, the F-coefficients in terms of Wigner 3j and 6j symbols, and the correction for finite-volume detectors through the attenuation coefficients Qk. Analysis is the working tool. About Me closes the app with the author's details.

In the Analysis tab the user declares the cascade: energy, character (E or M) and multipolarity L, L′ of each of the two transitions — with a mixing ratio δ when a transition is mixed — plus spin and parity of the initial, intermediate and final level. From this, k_max() decides whether the Legendre series truncates at k = 2 or k = 4, and A_kk() evaluates the theoretical coefficients exactly using sympy.physics.wigner's 3j and 6j symbols — symbolic angular-momentum algebra rather than tabulated values. The app immediately draws the theoretical W(θ) curve and a generated level-scheme diagram of the cascade alongside the numerical Akk.

The second half is the experimental fit. The user enters the detector attenuation coefficients Q2 and Q4 and uploads a CSV of measured data (theta, count, error_count, norm, error_norm). The effective W(θ), normalised at θ = π/2 and attenuated by Q2/Q4, is fitted to the points with scipy.optimize.curve_fit, returning A22 and A44 with the uncertainties taken from the covariance matrix. When one of the transitions is mixed, the app goes one step further and scans δ over the full arctan space in 10 000 steps, plotting the χ² curve to locate the minimum and then drawing the A22/A44 correlation ellipse — which is how the mixing ratio is actually extracted from data. Sample CSVs for the classic 152Eu and 60Co cascades ship with the app, and it is deployed publicly on Streamlit Community Cloud.

Key features

  • Exact theoretical coefficients — Akk is computed from Wigner 3j and 6j symbols via sympy.physics.wigner, so the F-coefficients are evaluated symbolically rather than interpolated from published tables.
  • Automatic series truncation — k_max = min(2I, L₁+L₁′, L₂+L₂′) is derived from the declared scheme, so the app knows on its own whether the correlation stops at k = 2 or extends to k = 4.
  • Mixed-multipolarity support — each transition can be declared pure or mixed (M+E); a mixed transition exposes its own δ and is handled correctly inside Ak.
  • Generated level scheme — the app draws the three-level decay diagram with spins, parities, transition energies and multipolarities directly from the inputs, as a figure you can drop into a report.
  • Experimental CSV fit — upload measured counts with their errors and normalisation; the effective W(θ) is fitted with scipy.optimize.curve_fit and A22, A44 are reported with 1σ uncertainties from the covariance matrix.
  • Finite-detector correction — user-supplied attenuation coefficients Q2 and Q4 enter the fitted function, so the comparison between theory and measurement is done on the effective correlation actually observed by real detectors.
  • χ² scan over the mixing ratio — δ is swept over 10 000 points across arctan space to map the χ² landscape and locate its minimum, the standard way of extracting δ from an angular correlation measurement.
  • A22–A44 correlation plot — the theoretical locus traced by varying δ is drawn against the fitted point and its error bars, giving a visual consistency check between the measurement and the assumed spin sequence.
  • Built-in theory reference — a full LaTeX derivation of the formalism, with bibliography, sits in the first tab so the tool is usable as teaching material and not just as a calculator.
  • Sample datasets — pre-labelled CSVs for 152Eu (220, 320, 342 keV) and 60Co cascades are bundled for testing and demonstration.
  • Public deployment — hosted on Streamlit Community Cloud, usable from a browser with no local install.

Tech stack

Python

The whole application, cleanly split into app.py (UI and input parsing only) and functions.py (every physics computation and figure), so the numerical core can be reused or tested independently of Streamlit.

Streamlit

Provides the three-tab layout, the widget grid for the decay scheme, LaTeX rendering of the formalism, the CSV uploader and the data table — and hosts the app publicly on Streamlit Community Cloud.

SymPy

sympy.physics.wigner supplies the Wigner 3j and 6j symbols behind the F-coefficients, giving exact angular-momentum algebra instead of tabulated approximations.

SciPy

optimize.curve_fit performs the weighted non-linear least-squares fit of the effective W(θ) to the experimental points and returns the covariance matrix used for the uncertainties.

NumPy

Evaluates the Legendre expansion, builds the 10 000-point δ grid for the χ² scan and handles all the array arithmetic behind the plots.

Matplotlib

Renders every figure the app produces: the theoretical W(θ), the generated level scheme, the fit against the experimental points, the χ² curve and the A22–A44 correlation.

pandas

Parses the uploaded CSV of experimental counts and displays it as an inspectable table before the fit runs.
