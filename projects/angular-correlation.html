Overview

Angular Correlation is a web application for computing, plotting and fitting γ-γ angular correlations in nuclear physics. When an excited nucleus de-excites through a cascade of two gamma rays, the direction of the second photon is not isotropic with respect to the first: the angular distribution encodes the spins of the nuclear levels and the character of the transitions.

The user describes the decay cascade — energies, multipolarities, mixing ratios and the spin and parity of the three levels — and the app draws the level scheme and the theoretical distribution. Experimental data can then be loaded to fit the correlation coefficients and extract the mixing ratio with its uncertainty.

The interface has three tabs: a typeset write-up of the theory that doubles as teaching material, the analysis workspace, and the report export.

From the theory to the measurement

Measurements arrive as an uploaded file or typed into an editable table, and are validated before any fit. The fit accounts for the finite size of real detectors, and the correlation between the two fitted coefficients is plotted against the theoretical locus as a visual consistency check of the assumed spin sequence.

When one transition is mixed, the mixing ratio is found by scanning chi-squared and refining the minimum, with a 1σ interval; when both are mixed, a heat map with confidence bands shows the two-parameter landscape, and the app flags degenerate cases.

What it does

Theory

  • Exact coefficients computed from Wigner 3j and 6j symbols, not interpolated from tables
  • Automatic truncation of the series from the declared level scheme
  • Pure and mixed multipolarities supported on each transition

Level scheme

  • Three-level decay diagram generated as a scalable vector graphic
  • Spins, parities, energies and multipolarities drawn from the inputs

Fit and statistics

  • Weighted least-squares fit with propagated errors
  • Finite-detector attenuation correction
  • Chi-squared scans for one or two mixing ratios with confidence intervals

Output

  • Multi-page PDF with inputs, curves, data table, fit results and every chart
  • Built-in theory reference with bibliography

How it is built

The project is split on purpose: the physics — Wigner algebra, non-linear fitting, chi-squared minimisation — lives in Python behind a small stateless API, while the React interface renders curves, tables and diagrams. This keeps the scientific core reusable and testable on its own, with formulas typeset by KaTeX and the PDF assembled in the browser.

Tech stack

React

The single-page front end: three tabs kept mounted so their state survives switching, the decay- scheme widget grid, the data table, and every chart re-rendered live as the inputs change.

FastAPI

Serves the two computation endpoints with Pydantic models validating spins, multipolarities and every uploaded data row before they reach the physics code.

SymPy

sympy.physics.wigner supplies the Wigner 3j and 6j symbols behind the F-coefficients, giving exact angular-momentum algebra instead of tabulated approximations.

SciPy

curve_fit for the weighted non-linear least squares, minimize_scalar and minimize to refine the χ² minimum, and brentq to solve the Δχ² = 1 crossings that set the uncertainty on δ.

NumPy

Evaluates the Legendre expansion, builds the arctan-δ grids and the outer products that make the two-dimensional χ² map cheap enough to compute per request.

Recharts & SVG

Recharts draws W(θ), the fit against the data and the χ² curve; the level scheme and the χ² heat map are hand-written SVG, since no chart library offers them.

KaTeX

Typesets the whole Theory tab and the formulas scattered through the Analysis tab, rendered synchronously in the browser with no MathJax-style layout shift.

jsPDF & PapaParse

PapaParse reads and writes the experimental CSVs; jsPDF with autoTable assembles the exported report, including the rasterised charts and an embedded font subset for Greek glyphs.

Vite & Tailwind CSS

Vite builds the front end, and Tailwind handles the layout and styling of the whole interface.
