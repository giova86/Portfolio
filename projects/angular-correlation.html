Angular Correlation

Analyzes and simulates the directional relationship between two emitted gamma rays in nuclear decay processes, visualizing the complex physics behind these phenomena.

Overview

Angular Correlation is a web application for computing, plotting and fitting γ-γ angular correlations in nuclear physics. When an excited nucleus de-excites through a cascade of two gamma rays, the direction of the second photon is not isotropic with respect to the first: the angular distribution W(θ) encodes the spins of the three nuclear levels involved and the multipolarities of the two transitions. The app turns that measurement into an interactive workflow — declare the decay scheme, get the theoretical correlation, feed in the experimental counts, and read off the fitted coefficients and the mixing ratio.

It is built as a React single-page front end on top of a FastAPI back end. The split is deliberate: the physics — Wigner algebra, non-linear fitting, χ² minimisation — stays in Python (physics.py, exposed through two endpoints, /api/theory and /api/fit), while everything the user touches is a React component that renders charts client-side with Recharts, hand-drawn SVG and KaTeX. The back end returns plain JSON — curves as arrays of points, coefficients as numbers — so no figure is ever rasterised on the server and every plot stays live and inspectable in the browser.

The interface has three tabs. Theory is a full write-up of the formalism typeset with KaTeX: the Legendre expansion of W(θ), the truncation rule for kmax, the Akk coefficients as a product of the two Ak, the mixing ratio δ, the F-coefficients in terms of Wigner 3j and 6j symbols, the finite-detector attenuation coefficients Qk, and the bibliography. Analysis is the working tool. About closes the app with the author's details.

In the Analysis tab the user declares the cascade: energy, character (E, M or M + E) and multipolarities L, L′ of each transition — with a mixing ratio δ when a transition is mixed — plus spin and parity of the initial, intermediate and final level. The back end's k_max() decides whether the Legendre series truncates at k = 2 or k = 4, and A_kk() evaluates the coefficients exactly with sympy.physics.wigner's 3j and 6j symbols — symbolic angular-momentum algebra rather than tabulated values. The theoretical W(θ) is redrawn as soon as an input changes, next to a level-scheme diagram generated as SVG straight from the inputs and the numerical Akk.

The second half is the experimental fit. Measurements arrive either as an uploaded CSV (theta, count, error_count, norm, error_norm, parsed with PapaParse) or typed into an editable table that can be exported back to CSV; both paths go through the same validator, which insists on a point at θ = 90° because the whole correlation is normalised there. Counts are normalised and their errors propagated, then the effective W(θ) — attenuated by the user's Q2/Q4 — is fitted with scipy.optimize.curve_fit, returning A22 and A44 with 1σ uncertainties from the covariance matrix.

Extracting δ is where the tool goes furthest. With one mixed transition, χ² is scanned over 10 000 points in arctan δ, the minimum refined with a bounded scalar minimisation so δ is not quantised by the grid, and the 1σ interval found by bracketing the two Δχ² = 1 crossings with Brent's method; the result is shown as a χ² curve plus the A22–A44 correlation locus with the fitted point on it. With both transitions mixed, χ² becomes a surface over (δ₁, δ₂): since Ak factorises, the 241×241 grid costs 2n Wigner evaluations instead of n², the best cell seeds a Nelder–Mead refinement, and the errors come from a profile-likelihood scan that re-minimises one δ while the other moves. The surface is drawn as a hand-built heat map with Δχ² ≤ 1/4/9 confidence bands — a chart type Recharts does not provide. The whole analysis can finally be exported as a multi-page PDF report, with the on-screen SVG charts rasterised into the document and an embedded DejaVu subset so Greek letters and subscripts survive the export.

Key features

  • Exact theoretical coefficients — Akk is computed from Wigner 3j and 6j symbols via sympy.physics.wigner, so the F-coefficients are evaluated symbolically rather than interpolated from published tables.
  • Automatic series truncation — k_max = min(2I, L₁+L₁′, L₂+L₂′) is derived from the declared scheme, so the app knows on its own whether the correlation stops at k = 2 or extends to k = 4.
  • Mixed-multipolarity support — each transition can be declared pure or mixed (M + E); a mixed transition exposes its own δ and is handled correctly inside Ak.
  • Generated level scheme — the three-level decay diagram, with spins, parities, transition energies and multipolarities, is drawn as inline SVG from the inputs, so it scales cleanly and can be lifted straight into a report.
  • CSV upload or manual entry — experimental points can be uploaded (PapaParse) or typed into an editable table and exported back to CSV; both feed the same validator, which checks every field and requires the θ = 90° normalisation point.
  • Weighted least-squares fit — the effective W(θ) is fitted with scipy.optimize.curve_fit on propagated per-point errors, reporting A22 and A44 with 1σ uncertainties from the covariance matrix.
  • Finite-detector correction — user-supplied attenuation coefficients Q2 and Q4 enter the fitted function, so theory and measurement are compared on the effective correlation actually observed by real detectors.
  • χ² scan with a refined minimum — δ is swept over 10 000 points in arctan space, the minimum polished by a bounded minimisation, and the 1σ interval located by solving Δχ² = 1 with Brent's method instead of reading it off the grid.
  • Two free mixing ratios — when both transitions are mixed, χ² is mapped over the (δ₁, δ₂) plane, refined with Nelder–Mead and turned into a heat map with Δχ² ≤ 1/4/9 confidence bands; uncertainties come from a profile-likelihood scan, and the app flags the degenerate case where two free parameters leave no degrees of freedom.
  • A22–A44 correlation plot — the theoretical locus traced by varying δ is drawn against the fitted point and its error bars, giving a visual consistency check between the measurement and the assumed spin sequence.
  • PDF report export — inputs, theoretical curve, data table, fit results and every chart are assembled into a multi-page PDF with jsPDF; the live SVG charts are rasterised on the client and an embedded DejaVu subset keeps Greek letters and sub/superscripts intact.
  • Built-in theory reference — a full KaTeX derivation of the formalism, with bibliography, sits in the first tab so the tool doubles as teaching material and not just a calculator.
  • Stateless JSON API — two POST endpoints (/api/theory, /api/fit) with Pydantic-validated payloads return numbers and curves, so the physics core is reusable outside the UI and testable on its own.

Tech stack

React

The single-page front end: three tabs kept mounted so their state survives switching, the decay- scheme widget grid, the data table, and every chart re-rendered live as the inputs change.

FastAPI

Serves the two computation endpoints with Pydantic models validating spins, multipolarities and every uploaded data row before they reach the physics code.

SymPy

sympy.physics.wigner supplies the Wigner 3j and 6j symbols behind the F-coefficients, giving exact angular-momentum algebra instead of tabulated approximations.

SciPy

curve_fit for the weighted non-linear least squares, minimize_scalar and minimize to refine the χ² minimum, and brentq to solve the Δχ² = 1 crossings that set the uncertainty on δ.

NumPy

Evaluates the Legendre expansion, builds the arctan-δ grids and the outer products that make the two-dimensional χ² map cheap enough to compute per request.

Recharts & SVG

Recharts draws W(θ), the fit against the data and the χ² curve; the level scheme and the χ² heat map are hand-written SVG, since no chart library offers them.

KaTeX

Typesets the whole Theory tab and the formulas scattered through the Analysis tab, rendered synchronously in the browser with no MathJax-style layout shift.

jsPDF & PapaParse

PapaParse reads and writes the experimental CSVs; jsPDF with autoTable assembles the exported report, including the rasterised charts and an embedded font subset for Greek glyphs.

Vite & Tailwind CSS

Vite builds the front end and proxies /api to uvicorn in development; Tailwind handles the layout and the utility-level styling of the whole interface.
